I’ve always been fascinated by James Cook — Captain Cook as we called him back then. It was a point of pride that, like me, he was born near Middlesbrough. On family days out we’d walk up to the Captain Cook monument on the Cleveland Hills. Family holidays were spent in Staithes and nearby Whitby, the historic port where he was an apprentice, and where they built the ship that would take him to the other side of the world.

But for all that, I never imagined I’d get any closer to him than reading about his discoveries and studying maps of his voyage. And yet in early 2026 I found myself — unexpectedly — on an island in the Pacific, in the city of Suva on Fiji, a chance to research the story of Cook’s explorations and to understand the consequences of his three voyages today. This is my Cook in the Pacific podcast.
